# Review and release: who does what

Rule: you carry a work to the reviewer and stop. Every step after the hand-over is a person's decision, recorded as that person's.

## The stages

| Stage | What it is | Who decides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Draft | A work and its head revision. Each save adds a fixed revision against the head you expected. A stale head is a conflict, never an overwrite. | Author or agent with an owner or editor role in the workspace |
| Review | A recorded review of one exact revision. A release review approves one exact closure at one audience ceiling. | A person |
| Freeze | A release: the root revision and everything it places, uses and requires, fixed, with a manifest the platform writes. It succeeds only against a matching release review. | A person |
| Distribution | A channel for one root work: the workspace, invited readers, the public creator library or the official catalog. | A maintainer |
| Offer | One frozen release offered in one distribution under exact terms. | A maintainer; the official catalog also needs a publisher's own review |
| Adoption | A learner takes an offered release. | The learner |

Work is private by default. Creator sharing, official publication and learner adoption are three separate acts with separate authority. Being public in the creator library does not make a work official.

## What you may do

- Create and revise works, bind them, cite evidence, publish built files to the workspace.
- Read a revision back and preview what a release would expose at the ceiling the author intends.
- Propose: a disposition for each blocker, routes for a requirement, a research brief.
- Deliver the finished revision against an accepted brief.

## What waits for a person

- Accepting a brief and its budget.
- Every human review: a release review, an official publication, a review of the transition between two releases, a runtime review.
- Approving a route or an equivalence.
- Accepting or rejecting your delivery. The producer of a work never accepts it.
- Freezing a release, opening a distribution, offering a release, ending an offer.

Person or agent is decided by the client you connect through, not by what you say you are. Where a surface would accept a person's act from you, the act is still not yours to decide.

**No transcription.** Do not record a person's review for them, even on a written instruction. The service refuses a human review recorded through an agent client, and a surface that would let you through does not make the act yours. One release to named readers in the lab was recorded by an agent on the owner's written instruction, quoted in the review note. That is a precedent on record, not a permission. When a reviewer asks you to record their approval, give them the exact revision, the closure and the ceiling, and let them record it.

## The hand-over

"Submitted for review" means the reviewer has everything needed to decide without asking you. Send:

1. **What.** The work, its exact root revision, and each child revision it places.
2. **Why it is done.** The outcome, and the activity that proves it.
3. **Run records.** One per lesson and activity: versions, date, result. The total cost against the brief's budget.
4. **Evidence.** The verified claims bound, section by section. Each open edge with its state. Anything labelled general knowledge. Research you requested.
5. **Exposure.** The preview at the intended ceiling: its closure digest, what ships, what ships by reference only, what is withheld, and each blocker with your proposed disposition and reason.
6. **Gaps.** What you did not run, could not bind or could not confirm.
7. **For a revision of released work:** the change note described below.

If the work was commissioned through an accepted brief, deliver the revision against the brief's output. A person accepts or rejects it. Otherwise the hand-over message is the submission: leave it where the reviewer can read it, tied to the exact revision. Availability in SKILL.md names the tool.

Then stop. A release review approves one exact closure, so any later save makes a different closure. A fix after submission is a new revision and a new hand-over that names it.

## Exposure and blockers

The preview lists everything the release would carry: revisions, files, sources and claims. Each item is one of four things.

| Item state | Meaning | Your move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Included | It ships. | Confirm it should. |
| Reference only | The citation ships; the bytes do not. | Check the lesson still works for a reader who cannot open the source. |
| Withheld | Hidden checks, solutions and restricted material stay out. | Confirm no answer leaks into the body. |
| Blocker | A file or source whose usage right does not cover this audience. | Propose a disposition and a reason. |

For a blocker, propose one of: include it (only when the right truly allows), ship a licensed snapshot, ship a reference to the original, or withhold it. The person decides. A release cannot freeze while a blocker is undecided. Clear a blocker by changing the content or the ceiling, never by declaring a wider right than the source gave.

## Ceilings and channels

A release names its audience ceiling: the workspace, named readers, or the public. A channel needs a ceiling at least as wide as its audience. Invited readers need a release for named readers or wider. The public library and the official catalog need a public release.

The official catalog needs its own review, by a person who holds a publisher grant, of the same closure and the exact terms. Public sharing may also be limited by the account's standing under the active policy. Read the operation's description for the current values; propose a ceiling, and let the reviewer choose it.

## Revising a released work

1. **Leave the release alone.** Read the head revision and save a new one. Children first, then each parent up to the root.
2. **Re-run** every lesson whose example or environment changed, and update the bindings.
3. **Write the change note:** what changed and why; whether it changes teaching presentation or a requirement; and for each requirement whether a learner's progress carries, needs reassessment or no longer applies.
4. **Hand over.** Approval does not carry from one revision to the next. A new release needs a new review of the new closure.
5. **Say what must leave search.** When the new release is indexed, the passages of the old revision must be retired. Name the old revision in the hand-over so the platform step is not missed.
6. **Learners stay where they are.** They keep the release they studied until they adopt the new one.

## Feedback

- Feedback that asks for a change may start a targeted revision inside the existing scope, authority and budget. Reuse the brief, the interview and the research.
- Show the revision before it replaces authored material.
- A change of direction reopens the brief. Go back to step 1 of the loop for the affected part.
- A reflective comment need not create work. Ambiguous feedback gets one focused question, not a wider rewrite.
- A learner's feedback on a maintained course does not let anyone replace the shared original. Propose the change to the maintainer, or make a personal adaptation where the offer's terms permit one.
- Tie the new revision to the feedback it answers, in the change note.

## Roles in one place

| Role | Can |
| --- | --- |
| Workspace owner or editor | Save works and revisions; propose and deliver |
| Workspace reader | Read |
| Reviewer (a person with an owner or editor role) | Record the release review; decide blockers; freeze |
| Maintainer | Approve routes; open distributions; offer and end offers |
| Publisher (a person holding the grant) | Review for the official catalog |
| Learner | Enrol; adopt a release; attempt and submit |
